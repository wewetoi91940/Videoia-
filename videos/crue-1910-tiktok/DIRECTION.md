# DIRECTION — crue-1910-tiktok

Strategy line: **This video tells young adults curious about history that in January 1910 the Seine stopped Paris —
down to its public clocks — and that the water's level is still marked on the city's walls.**
Destination: TikTok · 1080×1920 · 45 s · français

- **Core surprise:** a river stopped the city's clocks — an unexpected consequence that is true, sourced twice and
  unknown to most viewers (the barques are better known).
- **Payoff:** how it happened (the flooded compressed-air plant, frame 4), then the scale (8,62 m, frame 7), then the
  callback: the level is still engraved in Paris — "c'est là que l'eau s'est arrêtée" echoes "arrêté les horloges".
- **By 6 s the viewer knows:** Paris, January 1910, a flood after a very rainy winter.

**Chosen: #5 (unexpected consequence).** It is the most surprising true fact for this audience, it is clear without
sound, its payoff comes early (frame 4) and it allows a callback at the end. Honesty notes: the narration says
« horloges publiques » at frame 4; the first image is the flooded gare Saint-Lazare (28 January) without a punch-in on
its clock, so it never suggests that this particular clock is the evidence. **Runner-up for an A/B variant: #1.**
